## INFERRED — A/B decision reconstruction

**Point A (A01, A02, A03, A04):** The existing mattress had become an unreliable
path to restorative sleep, while buying another was an uncomfortable and risky project.
Evidence: E002 E004 E008 E010 E014 E025.

**Path Y:** Remain with the earlier mattress, experiment with how it was used,
research alternatives, and postpone replacement. Evidence: E004 E006 E008 E013 E014.

**Pre-switch baseline:** Reported waking discomfort and strain on family/work
function, but causal attribution to the mattress is not independently established.
Evidence: E002 E005 E007 E015.

**Tipping context:** An unexpected encounter with foam mattresses at Costco
occurred after active online research. His wife could touch a sample and join the
decision; he had a known store and prior return experience.
Evidence: E013 E017 E018 E019 E020 E027.

**Path X:** Purchase a more expensive foam mattress at Costco during a routine
family trip rather than ordering an unfamiliar Groupon option.
Evidence: E017 E019 E020 E022.

**Point B (B01):** Sleep well enough to function better as a worker, father,
and husband. Convenience and reversibility (B02/B03) are potential conditions
enabling the purchase, not substitutes for the desired life outcome.
Evidence: E015 E018 E020 E027.

**Observed Path X result:** **UNKNOWN** whether the purchase improved his sleep.
He still had and used the earlier mattress, and described the situation as unresolved.
Evidence: E023 E024 E026. Do not equate purchase with successful progress.

## SPECULATIVE — competing explanations and tests

**Hypothesis H1:** Perceived recovery from a failed purchase mattered more than the
lower Groupon price. Alternative: established retailer familiarity or social proof
was the decisive factor. Test: vary return logistics and seller familiarity
separately in a real, consented purchase opportunity. Evidence motivating the
hypothesis: E014 E020 E027.

**Hypothesis H2:** Spousal participation reduced hesitation. Alternative:
the physical sample mattered mainly for judging unfamiliar foam feel. Test:
observe joint and solo evaluation, keeping the sample experience constant.
Evidence motivating the hypothesis: E018 E019 E021.

**Hypothesis H3:** The apparent impulsive purchase was an accumulated, delayed
decision triggered by convenient discovery. Alternative: situational attention
during a stressful shopping trip was sufficient. Test: observe customers with
different histories of dissatisfaction, without assigning a population prevalence.
Evidence motivating the hypothesis: E008 E013 E016 E017.
