## The result in one paragraph

The participant wanted relief from poor sleep and its effect on daily functioning. He had been dissatisfied for about a year, tried to adapt the old mattress, postponed replacement during family/work/moving demands, researched foam alternatives, and abandoned a possible Groupon order because the failure-recovery path worried him. On an ordinary trip to Costco he encountered foam mattresses, touched a sample with his wife, obtained her assent, and bought a $699 option. He reported continuing to use and retain the older mattress afterward and said the mattress issue was unresolved. Therefore the **attempt to switch** is observed, but **successful progress toward better sleep is not established**.

| **Progress after purchase** | Clear end job is restoring sleep and daily function (`E015`) | Actual effect of the replacement not recorded; old bed still used | Follow actual use, retention, returns and function after delivery |

These mechanisms can interact. A buyer can value a sample *and* still fear an expensive return. One purchase does not identify their relative causal weights.

## 5. Product decision: what to do, and what not to build yet

**Recommendation (inferred): Test a genuinely reversible mattress trial, with tactile evaluation as a separate factor.** This is a recommendation for the **next experiment**, not an approved launch of pickup infrastructure.

A full-service return pickup may be valuable, but the interview does not establish that it would increase purchases. Likewise, sending foam samples to every buyer may waste effort if uncertain recovery is the real obstacle.

### The next discriminating experiment

Use a **real 2×2 randomized offer** with one seller and the same mattress at the same price. Recruit people who are actively considering replacing an unsatisfactory mattress; randomize at household/purchase-decision level.

| | No optional sample | Shared tactile foam sample |
|---|---|---|
| **Ordinary, clearly disclosed return path** | Control | Sample-only |
| **Verified, no-cost home pickup and refund after an unsatisfactory trial** | Pickup-only | Pickup + sample |

The seller must actually be able to honor either promise. Hold mattress, seller reputation, price, eligibility, shopping context where feasible, and trial period constant. Give participants an honest description of the terms; never simulate a fulfillment promise that cannot be kept.

**Primary measure:** Verified paid purchase attempts within a preregistered observation window—not sign-ups, intention ratings, or SSR scores. Follow actual delivery, use, completed returns, retained purchases and self-reported sleep/function separately. Record whether a household co-buyer took part, but don't infer consent from a click.

Before enrolling, define the smallest worthwhile effect, unit economics including pickups and returns, sample size/power, analysis, time horizon, attrition, and the decision threshold. Assess main effects and their interaction with uncertainty. If pickup is uneconomic, the implementation fails even if conversion improves.

This comparison can distinguish effects of pickup and sample **under the chosen retailer**. It does **not** isolate retailer trust from those conditions; a later study would be necessary to test seller identity or verified assurance separately.

### Decision rules

- **Pickup improves paid attempts and is economically viable:** Shape a narrow reversible-trial service, not a wholesale platform redesign.
- **Sample improves paid attempts but pickup does not:** Explore shared tactile evaluation without developing new return logistics.
- **Only the combination helps:** Treat the interaction as a possible bundled need; replicate before scaling.
- **Neither helps:** Re-examine trust, price, opportunity, the true desired progress, and whether the trial recruits the right job population.
- **Purchases rise but retained use or desired progress does not:** Do not label the job solved; revisit the product itself and the after-purchase experience.

These are **prospective conditional rules**, not observed findings.
