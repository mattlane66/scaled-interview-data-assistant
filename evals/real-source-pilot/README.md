# Two unfamiliar real switch interviews — method-execution pilot

**2026-11-22 | Current assistant source-read pilot | NOT a blind Codex/Claude evaluation, not human-approved.**

This pilot exercises the transcript-first research contract on published workshop interviews that were not used to build the canonical mattress demonstration: a [feature-phone to iPhone switch](https://jobstobedone.org/radio/iphone-jobs-to-be-done-interview/) and a [phone/GoPro to mirrorless-camera switch](https://jobstobedone.org/news/jobs-to-be-done-camera-interview/). The repository stores only URLs, source-located paraphrases and derived provisional interpretations; the original copyrighted transcripts are not copied.

**Source coverage:** PARTIAL inspected website ranges. Findings are not represented as full transcript audits. Material quoted summaries and interviewers' causal claims were **not** treated as additional observed customers. The analyst chose, read, interpreted and checked these sources in the same session; no independent blind reviewer approved the findings.

## Case 1 — iPhone purchase

| Element | Provisional reconstruction |
| --- | --- |
| **A** | A cheap, familiar feature-phone worked for basic communication but its use became socially uncomfortable and prepaid account problems accumulated |
| **Path Y** | Keep the inexpensive prepaid device, postpone a more expensive contract, avoid pressured stores |
| **Tipping sequence** | An uncomfortable social event plus a later free Saturday morning to research independently |
| **Path X** | iPhone on a carrier contract, after comparison, a waived upfront price and a callback |
| **Desired B** | Make the change without sacrificing autonomy or risking a complicated unfamiliar phone; social acceptance and ease appear important |
| **Outcome** | Purchase and some later social-media/blogging use are reported; durable social progress and satisfaction were not verified |

**Material nuance:** The participant explicitly said the earlier phone met calling/texting needs. Her account of increased social use came later, and she was unsure whether it originally drove the choice. A purely functional feature-improvement job is therefore inadequate. The facilitator's explanatory commentary about consumer motives is *not* direct participant evidence.

## Case 2 — Mirrorless camera purchase

| Element | Provisional reconstruction |
| --- | --- |
| **A** | Desired expressive travel videos were difficult with phone/GoPro, while conventional camera gear seemed burdensome |
| **Path Y** | Continue with phone and GoPro; describe travel experiences verbally |
| **Tipping sequence** | Experience using professional camera equipment, travel disappointment and subsequent plans; the exact role of seeing her sister is **contested by the participant** |
| **Path X** | Compact mirrorless camera, expert selection advice, repeated retailer attempts, successful alternative seller |
| **Desired B** | Produce compelling travel/personal video stories that fit a light-travel lifestyle |
| **Outcome** | Purchase and limited later filming discussion; sustained progress and full use history unverified |

**Material nuance:** A workshop interviewer probed whether seeing her sister caused the purchase, and the participant later questioned that inference. The analyst must preserve this as a **counterexample to an overconfident trigger claim** rather than copy the interviewer's narrative.

## Cross-case decision

These are two distinct **past purchase episodes**, not a representative sample, and do not share an established job cluster. The right action is **separate A→B analyses first**, with potential cross-domain *hypotheses* only. A compiled Behavioral Mechanism Map would require source reviews and a defended comparison scope. There is no empirical basis for calling either person an advanced Lead User in the methodological sense or predicting future actions.
